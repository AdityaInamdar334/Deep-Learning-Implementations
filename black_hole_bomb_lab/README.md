# Black-Hole Bomb: A Reproducible Nonlinear-Dynamics Lab

**A companion to the article on Švančara et al. (2026), _End state of the experimental black-hole bomb_.**

This lab teaches the difference between (1) a linear unstable mode, (2) a generic nonlinear saturated mode, (3) a *hypothesized* route from a pumped primary mode into secondary modes and damping, and (4) coherent wave coupling. It is CPU-only and does not require experimental data, a black hole, or helium.

**Provenance:** The experimental growth rate, final radially averaged amplitude, mode frequency, uncertainty values, and apparatus numbers are transcribed from the cited preprint. **Every plotted time series is synthetic and generated by simplified equations chosen for teaching.** In particular, the reduced Stuart–Landau and energy-transfer models are **not** equations fitted by the original authors, nor do they establish the causal origin of saturation. The paper's raw data are available upon reasonable request to the authors, not bundled here.

## Experiments

1. **Linear versus saturated growth.** Uses the published growth rate Γ = 0.081 ± 0.001 s⁻¹ and plateau 3.06 ± 0.05 mm, with an arbitrary initial seed of 20 µm. The toy nonlinear envelope follows `dA/dt = ΓA − βA³`, with β set to obtain the published plateau; this is an illustrative normal form, not a discovery about the mechanism.
2. **Harmonic generation.** Uses illustrative `A2 ∝ A1²` and static deformation `A0 ∝ A1²`. During early exponential growth, the second harmonic has twice the growth rate. This is a prediction of a quadratic response and consistent with the paper's phenomenology, but the coefficients are not fitted.
3. **Energy-transfer hypothesis.** Models primary proxy energy `P` with input `2ΓP`, transferring `λP²` to a secondary reservoir `S`, which loses `δS`. This makes the transfer-and-dissipation hypothesis explicit. The paper **did not** establish an experimental energy budget or prove that this is the unique saturation mechanism.
4. **Phase-locked coupling.** A synthetic bispectral/bicoherence control compares a harmonic locked to the fundamental's phase against an unrelated-phase harmonic. **This is not the paper's connected g₃ or g₄ correlator analysis**; it is an accessible way to understand why finding a frequency peak is weaker than detecting coherent mixing.
5. **Spatial mode visualization.** Combines m=1, m=2, and static m=0 terms into a synthetic azimuthal surface wave; not an imaging reconstruction.
6. **Circulation.** Converts the fitted circulation into ≈121,000 quanta using the helium circulation quantum; does not simulate microscopic vortex lines.

## Paper numbers and numerical caution

- Experimental Γ = **0.081 ± 0.001 s⁻¹**.
- Experimental saturated radially-averaged primary amplitude = **3.06 ± 0.05 mm**.
- Transition near **80 s** in the measured run.
- Measured m=1 frequency ≈**3.47 Hz**; perturbative model ≈**3.44 Hz**; WKB estimate ≈**3.97 Hz**.
- Cumulative nonlinear frequency drift magnitude ≈**0.098 Hz**.
- Helium temperature **1.70 ± 0.01 K**; cylinder radius **37.3 mm**, fluid depth **21.6 mm**, drain diameter **10 mm**.
- κ = **9.98×10⁻⁸ m²/s**, fitted C ≈ **19.2×10⁻⁴ m²/s**, corresponding to ≈**121,000** circulation quanta.
- Raw interface data: **not included**. The paper states data are available upon reasonable request.

This project is not an official University of Nottingham reproduction. It does not solve the experiment's full nonlinear free-surface PDE, assess analogue metric validity, or compute the authors' high-order connected spectral correlators.

## Experiment ideas

- Change the toy seed amplitude and watch the onset time shift, even though Γ and the plateau are fixed.
- Change the secondary damping and compare the timing of energy storage and release. Notice that fitting one plateau does not uniquely identify the mechanism.
- Change the coefficient of the harmonic response: its early log slope stays twice that of the primary even though its absolute size changes.
- Rerun the bicoherence control with different random seeds and sample sizes; phase-incoherent peaks can appear in a spectrum but do not remain coherently phase-coupled across segments.
