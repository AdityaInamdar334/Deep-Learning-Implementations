# References and provenance

## Primary preprint

Švančara, P., et al. (2026). **End state of the experimental black-hole bomb**. arXiv:2609.05595v1. https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05595v1

Measured values used as constants are in the Results and Methods (the growth-rate/amplitude paragraph near Fig. 1, frequency discussion near Fig. 2, and Methods parameter section). The paper reports frequency shifts, harmonic generation, and connected three- and four-wave correlations. It **hypothesizes** a nonlinear energy-drain mechanism and explicitly leaves detailed proof to future work. Experimental data are available upon reasonable request, not openly bundled.

## Related, important distinction about first demonstration

Cromb et al. (2025). **Creation of a black hole bomb instability in an electromagnetic system**. *Science Advances* 11, eadz4595. https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adz4595 . The first experimental electromagnetic analogue preceded the 2026 helium experiment. Its instability stopped when rotor energy dropped below the superradiant threshold, unlike the nonlinear steady state studied here.

## A note on all plots

Published measurements are the target values we use to illustrate a plausible toy trajectory. We **do not have the paper's raw time series**. The dynamic equations, noise distributions, coupling strengths, seed amplitude, and loss constants in this package were chosen as teaching examples; they must not be cited as the paper's fitted physical model or evidence identifying its saturation mechanism.
